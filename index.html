01 — Approach

Good design is not decoration. It is the shortest path between a person and what they came to do — built once, documented properly, and beautiful enough that the team defends it.

  1. ’14

    The only designer in the room

    First job: a six-person agency where I was the entire design department. Terrifying, and the best education available.

    @agencydays 12 years ago
    2014
    @agencydays

    The only designer in the room

    Nobody told me which problems were mine, so all of them were. Logos in the morning, checkout flows after lunch, arguing with printers by five. I learned to ship, to ask developers dumb questions early, and to never fall in love with a first draft. Most of what I charge for today was learned in that room.

  2. ’16

    Falling for systems

    I rebuilt the same button for the fortieth time and something snapped. My first pattern library followed within a month.

    @patternlibrary 10 years ago
    2016
    @patternlibrary

    Falling for systems

    It was rough — a sticker sheet with delusions of grandeur — but the team stopped debating paddings and started debating problems. That trade, decoration for decisions, is still the reason I love design systems. The library outlived my time there by years, which taught me what shipping something durable feels like.

  3. ’18

    Learning to love density

    Two years inside a trading platform taught me that white space is a luxury and hierarchy is oxygen.

    @tradingdesk 8 years ago
    2018
    @tradingdesk

    Learning to love density

    Traders do not want friendly. They want forty numbers on screen with zero ambiguity about which one is moving. I learned tabular design the hard way — density systems, states nobody demos, tables that survive a bad day in the markets. It permanently recalibrated what I consider a hard interface problem.

  4. ’20

    Research changes everything

    Eighteen shadowing sessions in clinics. The software wasn't broken — it was designed around the wrong people.

    @clinicteam 6 years ago
    2020
    @clinicteam

    Research changes everything

    Watching a nurse re-enter the same vitals into three systems ended any romance I had about designing from a desk. Since then every engagement starts in the field if I can get there. The insight rate per hour of shadowing embarrasses every workshop I have ever run.

  5. ’22

    Going out on my own

    First retainer signed. Suddenly every decision — pricing, process, positioning — was mine, which is the point.

    @studioofone 4 years ago
    2022
    @studioofone

    Going out on my own

    Independence forced me to treat my own practice like a product: a niche, a process people can buy, and standards nobody else enforces. Half of my clients since have come through engineers I once handed specs to. That referral chain means more to me than any award on the shelf.

  6. ’24

    The ninth design system

    By now I have built nine. The lesson: a system is a product with users, politics and a roadmap — not a Figma file.

    @designsystems 2 years ago
    2024
    @designsystems

    The ninth design system

    The artifacts are the easy part. Governance is the design problem: who owns a token, who reviews a component, what happens when marketing wants a special. The systems that survived are the ones where we designed the org around the library, not just the library.

  7. ’26

    The journey continues

    A new monogram, a new site, and the same obsession. Currently exploring where AI genuinely helps a design practice.

    @mbstudio this year
    2026
    @mbstudio

    The journey continues

    The mark you see on this site is the first identity I have ever designed for myself — the hardest client I have had. Next: deeper motion work, more research-led engagements, and finding the places where new tools sharpen craft instead of flattening it. The best work is still ahead.
